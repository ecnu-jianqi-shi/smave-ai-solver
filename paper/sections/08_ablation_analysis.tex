\section{Ablation and Cost Analysis}
\label{sec:ablation}

\subsection{Component-Level Ablation}

On a four-stage synthetic contract, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cascade-index} matches the
minimum over all 24 permutations. This checks fixed-cascade ordering, not
probability calibration or globally optimal subset selection.

The component matrix isolates router, candidate, correction, gate, and continuation on
one 64-scenario linear workload. Forcing the operator while
retaining correction and gate separates expert quality from router quality. A
finite but deliberately bad candidate is rejected and reaches the deterministic terminal
solver after the configured correction limit. Tampered expert, certificate, and
RuntimeBundle artifacts are rejected by the production solve path rather than only
by an offline checker.

Removing the gate would make the nonlinear operator appear orders of magnitude
faster because raw inference takes approximately $0.58\,\mu$s, but the maximum
normalized state error exceeds 597. Removing correction would therefore convert a
profitable verified path into a fast wrong answer. Conversely, applying the wrong
corrector preserves the acceptance test but yields 0\% acceptance and no acceleration. These
two ablations distinguish correctness from usefulness.

The correction-budget sweep exposes a second mechanism-level ablation. On the linear
family, budget zero already passes every original-equation test. On the nonlinear
family, moving from zero to one correction step raises acceptance only to
\NonlinearBudgetOneAcceptancePercent\%, whereas two steps reach full acceptance and
reduce candidate-inclusive complete cost to
\NonlinearBudgetTwoVsZeroCostRatio{}$\times$ the zero-step path. The relevant frontier
is therefore discrete and family-dependent: too little correction pays for rejection
and classical continuation, while unnecessary correction adds cost after acceptance
has saturated. A production profile--Router--Newton probe confirms the mechanism: the
same nonlinear candidate continues to the terminal solver at budget zero but is accepted after two Newton
steps at budget two; this is not a universal two-step optimum.

For three actions over two experts, the joint DP selects
$(e_B,1)\rightarrow(e_A,2)$ at $\JointRouteSelectedCost\,\mu$s, matches the exhaustive
oracle, costs $\JointRouteVsBestSingle\times$ the best single action, and executes the
gate-verified rejection--continuation--acceptance path. Both fixed-seed
$\JointRoutePropertyCases$-case independent and $\JointRouteInteractionCases$-case
interaction sweeps have zero oracle gap; interactions change
$\JointRouteInteractionChanged$ plans, and all $\JointRouteHardnessGraphs$ graphs satisfy
Proposition~\ref{prop:interaction-hardness}. Planning-only states/transitions are
$\JointScalingProductionStates$/$\JointScalingProductionTransitions$ for the deployed
$\JointScalingProductionActions$-action, $k=3$ shape and
\num{\JointScalingMeasuredStates}/\num{\JointScalingMeasuredTransitions} for the largest
executed $\JointScalingMeasuredActions$-action, $k=6$ profile. Under the one-million cap,
exact preflight admits \JointScalingCapAdmittedActions{} actions at
\num{\JointScalingCapAdmittedStates} states but rejects \JointScalingCapRejectedActions{}
at \num{\JointScalingCapRejectedStates} before any DP-state visit. These are optimizer/control
checks, not transfer, timing, or workload-speedup evidence.

\subsection{Fusing a Strict Gate}

Reorganizing the same FP64 original-expression calculation yields
$\FusedLinearGateSpeedup\times$ and $\FusedNonlinearGateSpeedup\times$ gate speedup
over 100 repetitions of 65 requests, with lower bounds \FusedLinearGateLower{} and
\FusedNonlinearGateLower{} and identical decisions/residuals. Verification can
therefore be optimized without weakening acceptance, although full-solve impact
remains workload-dependent because candidate and corrector cost can dominate.

\subsection{Router Regret and Model Complexity}

More complex selectors do not automatically improve deployable routing. The
nearest-neighbor and depth-3 CART baselines narrow practical regret, but winner labels
and selected experts vary with microsecond timing noise; a structure-derived selector
also changes rank across runs. We therefore report regret, not winner accuracy, and
retain a gate-filtered top-$k$ cascade. Control-aware abstention reduces v6 from raw
$\SuiteRouteRawRegret\times$ to $\SuiteRouteRegretPrecise\times$, but switches no
frozen v5 request; v6 alone would overstate robustness.

\subsection{Where Time Goes}

Raw candidates are only \LinearCandidateSharePercent\% and
\NonlinearCandidateSharePercent\% of layered full-path time; correction plus Runtime
gate accounts for \LinearCorrectionGateSharePercent\% and
\NonlinearCorrectionGateSharePercent\%. Generic correction reaches only
$\LinearSharedBaselineSpeedup\times$/$\NonlinearSharedBaselineSpeedup\times$, versus
$\LinearOperatorSpeedup\times$/$\NonlinearOperatorSpeedup\times$ for complete paths;
specialized recurrence, FFT, SIMD, and batched gates also drive PDEBench gains. Thus
the target is fusion, not inference alone.
