\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Classical Solver Ecosystems}

Sparse direct and Krylov methods remain the correctness and robustness foundation
for numerical simulation. GMRES handles nonsymmetric systems~\cite{saad1986gmres};
SuperLU exposes sparse LU factorizations~\cite{li2005superlu}.
SUNDIALS provides nonlinear, ODE, and DAE solvers with mature integration
contracts~\cite{hindmarsh2005sundials}, while PETSc organizes portable solver
composition~\cite{balay1997petsc}. These
systems demonstrate the value of composable backends, but they generally require a
user or fixed policy to choose among algorithms. \system builds on them as
authoritative experts rather than attempting to replace their numerical semantics.

\subsection{Learned PDE and Equation Solvers}

DeepONet and Fourier neural operators learn mappings between function spaces
~\cite{lu2021deeponet,li2021fno}. PDEBench provides a multi-family benchmark for
scientific machine learning~\cite{takamoto2022pdebench}. These approaches can
produce very fast inference for fixed families, but their reported error and
speedup contracts vary in predicted quantity, hardware, training cost, and whether
the original equation is checked per request.

A complementary line learns components inside an iterative method. Learned
preconditioners can reduce conjugate-gradient time while the iterative residual
remains authoritative~\cite{li2023preconditioner}. FCG-NO places a
resolution-transferable neural operator inside flexible conjugate gradients as a
nonlinear preconditioner~\cite{rudikov2024fcgno}. \system generalizes this
principle to a portfolio: learned warm starts, inverse actions, multigrid cycles,
and full-state operators may all propose candidates, but their role and correction
path are decided per equation family.

Recent hybrid methods make the comparison closer. HINTS interleaves a DeepONet
with relaxation to balance error modes in an iterative PDE solver
~\cite{zhang2024hints}. The Greedy PDE Router chooses neural or classical updates
at each iteration to optimize the error trajectory
~\cite{rayan2025greedy}. Error-Conditioned Neural Solvers instead feed the
residual field to a learned corrector and note that small residual need not imply
small reconstruction error in ill-conditioned systems~\cite{jiang2026ens}.
\system asks which typed pipeline remains profitable after every stage and rejection
is charged. Its gate is mandatory final acceptance under the declared equation and
constraint tolerance, not a proxy for unavailable ground-truth error.

\subsection{Algorithm Portfolios and Mixtures of Experts}

Rice's algorithm-selection formulation maps problem features to algorithms and
performance~\cite{rice1976}; later surveys identify feature cost, distribution
shift, and runtime overhead as central concerns~\cite{kotthoff2014}.

Learned numerical-solver selection is also established. Lighthouse combines solver
taxonomies with performance classification~\cite{jessup2016lighthouse}. Recent work
adds matrix/right-hand-side representations~\cite{zhang2026spectra}, run-local adaptation
~\cite{zabegaev2026datadriven}, and regret guarantees for sequential SOR tuning
~\cite{khodak2024relax}. We therefore claim neither request conditioning,
unseen-matrix prediction, nor online solver adaptation as unique.

Cost-per-success ordering is also prior art: independent early-stop tests admit
ratio orderings, including extensions with precedence constraints
~\cite{berend2014tests}. We use that ordering as a lemma rather than a novelty
claim; Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-finite-cascade} instead states the exactness
boundary of our finite one-budget-per-expert recurrence.

Multimode diagnosis already uses dynamic programming when mode changes alter later
test costs~\cite{ruan2004multimode}. Our model keeps only adjacent cost effects and
history-independent acceptance; neither transition-aware sequencing nor DP is new.

Numerical solving introduces an additional constraint: a router decision is not
itself evidence that a candidate satisfies the equation. \system therefore uses
``gate'' in the numerical sense of an acceptance test computed from the original
equation, distinct from the learned routing gate in a conventional MoE. The router may
be wrong without making the returned answer wrong; a bad decision increases cost or
continues to another numerical path, because the original-equation gate determines
acceptance.

\subsection{Positioning}

HINTS and the Greedy PDE Router contribute iteration-level hybrids; solver selectors
contribute request features and adaptation. \system instead studies a typed request-level
cascade, a reach-weighted objective charging rejection and terminal fallback, and a
return invariant that routing cannot bypass original-equation acceptance. The dated
search audit supports no ``first'' claim for this combination or for the underlying
ratio ordering.
