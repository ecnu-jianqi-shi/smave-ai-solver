\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Research Questions}

The evaluation addresses five research questions.

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1:] Does one verified runtime cover heterogeneous equation families
  without hiding failures or unsupported comparisons?
  \item[RQ2:] When does complete-cost expert fusion accelerate repeated solves?
  \item[RQ3:] Does calibrated routing improve over a fixed expert, jointly select
  correction budget, and remain effective under workload shift?
  \item[RQ4:] How do correction and original-equation verification affect learned
  candidates?
  \item[RQ5:] What do complete-cost decomposition, strict-gate, parallelism, and
  device-placement studies reveal about the remaining bottlenecks?
\end{description}

\subsection{Platform and Build}

The authoritative results in this draft were generated on an Apple M4 ARM64 system
running macOS 26.5.2, Apple Clang 21.0.0, CMake 4.3.2, and a Release build. CPU
paths use FP64 unless a device candidate explicitly declares another precision;
all final equation gates are evaluated with the path-specific published contract,
and the primary runtime contract uses CPU FP64 original expressions. Because this
is one machine and software stack, we treat performance portability as an open
threat rather than extrapolating to Linux/x86-64 or CUDA.

The seven PDEBench-derived assets in Table~\ref{tab:suites} use designated classical
baselines on identical inputs and accuracy contracts. Complete time includes setup,
candidate, correction, and gate. Each workload has one warm-up and 30 process-level
repetitions with alternating workload order and fixed inference threads. A separate
probe alternates classical-first and \system-first phase order over 30 matched pairs;
run-level total-runtime ratios, not within-run solves, are the inference unit.

The linear and 16-variable nonlinear operator studies use 16 training and 64
disjoint held-out scenarios, repeated in 100 batches (6,400 Runtime requests).
A nonsymmetric Helmholtz replication is retained as a negative transfer result.

Routing transfer uses a separate 5$\times$5 family with 64 scenarios and 20
repetitions per axis: a source diagonal of $4.5$, a conditioning shift to $3.5$,
and a topology shift with west/east coefficients $1.35/0.65$. Production competition
and family-router paths include all candidate, gate, trace, and continuation cost; metrics
cover eligible-expert calibration/rank, regret, speedup, status changes, and misroutes.

Both primary operator families also use one shared transparent hybrid control. It
reuses the same fitted FP64 latent candidate and held-out scenarios, applies at most
32 weighted diagonal-residual Jacobi corrections, invokes a strict original-equation
acceptance gate, and sends every rejection to the original classical
solver. Candidate inference, correction, both gates, trace persistence, and continuation
are included in the paired complete-runtime measurement. This control is a common
algorithmic baseline, not an implementation of an external published method.

The HINTS baseline pins official revision
\texttt{0c8b712f81ed08bdf27c3a215f8edb99910f5e2f}~\cite{zhang2024hints}, pretrained
weights, all 750 Poisson cases, 400 updates, the 25:1 schedule, and $\omega=2/3$.
Both methods solve the exported 29-unknown system under the same FP64 normalized
residual. Five runs follow three warm-ups; setup and HINTS diagnostics are excluded
from online time. \system{} uses its unmodified production path.

After headline timing, the production corrector is swept over
$\{0,1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ for 30 rotated repetitions of all 64 held-out requests.
Every point includes candidate, correction, gate, trace, and continuation; the stable
mechanism result is the minimum budget attaining full acceptance, not the timing winner.

For joint routing, three calibrated actions span two nonlinear experts with $k=2$.
An independent enumerator checks every valid subset, budget, and order; production
Runtime must reproduce the selected rejection--continuation--acceptance path. To test
the finite-action recurrence beyond that probe, a fixed-seed property sweep constructs
256 six-action cases (three experts, two budgets each), cycles $k$ from one to three,
and exhaustively enumerates subsets and permutations. A second 256-case sweep adds 24
adjacent multipliers; all 4,096 directed four-vertex graphs check the hardness reduction.
A prefrozen planning-only counter study spans up to 16 experts plus the deployed shape.
For profile transfer, two
12-variable nonlinear SCC families each freeze Runtime-trace
profiles on 32 scenarios and report regret against an exhaustive oracle on 32 disjoint
held-out scenarios; this is not a workload-speedup measurement.

The request-conditioned extension has three 12-variable nonlinear families, three
experts, four budgets each, and $k=3$ (12 actions). Runtime traces provide 192/96/192
training/calibration/held-out requests with three repetitions per action. Fixed ridge
and logistic models are evaluated by cost error, Brier/calibration error, realized
per-request oracle regret, static/fixed controls, plan diversity, exact-DP agreement,
and production gate/continuation outcomes; held-out data never enter fitting or calibration.

The public extension uses fixed six/four-matrix training/calibration sets, eight
RHS/tolerance requests per matrix, and a 19-action PCG/GMRES/direct union. Three
development folds establish the control-aware anchor rule without final-cohort data.
Each final cohort contains three matrix-ID- and collection-group-disjoint matrices,
one from each numeric class, and each compatible action is measured five times under
a same-request counterbalanced terminal reference. Evaluation covers cost/pass
prediction, exhaustive realized regret, control ablations, attempt order,
independent original-matrix acceptance, terminal continuation, and action interaction.

The protocol preserves all final outcomes. V4 is contract-invalid because its frozen
verifier asserted 48 requests; v5 is a valid negative first run. After the guard and
v6 package were frozen from development evidence, v6 was executed exactly once. A
deterministic v5 counterfactual applies the current C++ anchor comparison to frozen
tables with no solver execution. V5/v6 selection, payload, source, executable,
verifier, and output-path contracts precede timing; no further cohort is searched. A
post-hoc zero-execution audit uses held-out action statuses only for support-shift diagnosis.

The intra-node study reuses 40 Navier--Stokes Helmholtz solves at $1,2,4,8,$ and
$10$ workers. Each point has one warm-up and 30 alternating-order repetitions;
Accelerate uses one internal thread. Fixed-seed 10,000-resample intervals use paired
speedup ratios.

The batch study fixes ten workers and increases same-dataset requests from 12 to 160
solves per process under the same protocol. Throughput is solves divided by complete
verified time and normalized to 12 solves.

A second worker study isolates the fused gate on 2,080 linear and 2,080 nonlinear
requests per repetition. It includes thread creation, alternates worker order over 30
repetitions, and compares every decision/residual with a sequential reference. It
measures gate throughput, not complete-solve scaling.

\subsection{Baselines and Comparisons}

Classical baselines are workload-specific and include structured direct solvers,
PCG with classical preconditioners, restarted GMRES with ILUT/ILU(0), sparse direct
backends, finite-difference Newton, and original model solvers. For routing, we
compare a fixed expert, the online rule, a calibrated profile, simple feature
selectors, and a hindsight per-scenario best gate-passing expert. The hindsight oracle is
not deployable: it excludes search cost and uses query outcomes, so it is reported
only as a reference for regret. The final SuiteSparse hierarchy separately reports
the raw conditioned model, training-family fixed anchor, current control-aware anchor,
global fixed SuperLU, static profile, and realized per-request oracle. These names are
not interchangeable: the current policy may abstain to global fixed, while an offline
replay can test that decision rule without producing new timing observations.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Contract}

The primary performance metric is paired complete-runtime speedup
\begin{equation}
 S=\operatorname{median}_i \frac{T_{\mathrm{baseline},i}}
 {T_{\system,i}}.
\end{equation}
Where repeated paired samples are available, reports include a fixed-seed
bootstrap 95\% interval and paired win rate. A speedup claim is considered stable
only when the interval lower bound exceeds one. We additionally report residual or
QoI error, acceptance, continuation, failure, timeout, no-common-success, gate mismatch,
training time, and break-even queries.

For the PDEBench-derived study, each paired sample is the baseline-to-\system total
runtime ratio from one measured process run. The reported median, fixed-seed
10,000-resample bootstrap interval, and run win rate are computed over 30 such
ratios. The counterbalanced all-workload probe reports the paired ratio between
\system-first and classical-first speedups for each of seven workloads. We summarize
the per-workload median ratio and fixed-seed 10,000-resample paired bootstrap
interval; this quantifies order sensitivity under two complete phase orders rather
than proving order invariance. No per-solve independence is assumed. Router,
operator, and gate studies likewise retain their declared paired repetition units
and use the interval rule above.

For a run with zero observed erroneous accepts in $n$ independent trials, the
one-sided exact upper bound at confidence $1-\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
 p_{\mathrm{upper}}=1-\alpha^{1/n}.
\end{equation}
The operator validations count 64 independent held-out scenarios, not 6,400 timing
repetitions, and hence report a 95\% upper bound of 4.57\% after zero observed
erroneous accepts. This conservative scenario-level statistic is separate from the
deterministic residual decision for each candidate and is not a claim of zero risk.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Claim Extraction}

Every manuscript value is tied to a machine-generated report. The normalized core
archive rebuilds Release, passes 29/29 tests, reruns solver evidence checks, and
rebuilds the PDF; large benchmark payloads remain externally acquired.
